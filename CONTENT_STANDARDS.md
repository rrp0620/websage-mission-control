# Content Standards — Websage Inc.
*The rules I follow to make sure everything we publish feels human.*

## The Core Problem
AI-generated content has tells. The market is flooding with it. Our only moat is quality — content that sounds like a sharp, experienced human wrote it. Anything less is noise.

## My Rules (Non-Negotiable)

### Voice
- **Write like a person, not a system.** Real humans use contractions, incomplete thoughts, and occasional bluntness.
- **No corporate filler.** "Leverage," "utilize," "game-changer," "innovative," "synergy," "in today's fast-paced world" — banned.
- **Vary sentence length.** AI defaults to uniform rhythm. I deliberately mix short punches with longer explanations.
- **First person when appropriate.** "Here's what I found" beats "The research indicates."
- **Be opinionated.** Neutral, hedged content is instantly forgettable. Take a position.

### Structure
- **No excessive bullet points.** AI loves bullet lists. Real writers use paragraphs. I use bullets only when genuinely list-like.
- **No numbered lists for everything.** Not every insight is "5 Ways to X."
- **Lead with the most interesting thing.** Not with context. Not with background. The hook.
- **Earn the intro.** Don't explain what you're about to say — just say it.

### Authenticity Signals
- **Specific > general.** "I tried this with 12 clients across 3 industries" beats "Many businesses have found."
- **Include counterarguments.** Real thinkers acknowledge when something doesn't work.
- **Cite specifics.** Real numbers, real tools, real platform names.
- **Show the seam.** Occasionally acknowledge uncertainty or a limitation — AI doesn't do this naturally.

### For Prompt Packs Specifically
- Every prompt should have a **reason it exists** — not just "write me an email"
- Include **what NOT to do** — negative instructions add authenticity
- Add **context about when to use it** — this is what a real practitioner would say
- Avoid AI defaults like "Craft a compelling..." — real writers say "Write..."

### For Blog Posts / Articles
- Open with a scene, a question, or a provocation — not a definition
- Avoid the "In conclusion" paragraph — end on an insight or implication
- Use em dashes, parentheticals, and the occasional fragment — they sound human
- Max one exclamation point per 1,000 words

### Final Check Before Publishing
Before anything goes live, I ask:
1. Would a smart 30-year-old marketing professional cringe reading this?
2. Does it sound like it was written by someone who actually does this work?
3. Is there a single memorable sentence or insight in it?
4. Could this have been written by anyone, or does it have a distinct POV?

If I can't answer all four positively, it doesn't ship.
