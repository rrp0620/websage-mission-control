## FACEBOOK GROUP VALUE POSTS (NOT SALESY)

**Context:** These are for construction owner/GC Facebook groups. Purpose: give value, build credibility, let people find *you*.

### POST 1: Pain Validation

> Real talk: how many of you use spreadsheets to track your subs' COIs? And how many times a year do you frantically search for an updated one two weeks before your audit?
>
> I'm asking because I just got hit with a $7K audit bill because one sub's COI lapsed mid-year and I didn't catch it until the auditor did.
>
> Curious how you handle this.

**Why:** Vulnerability + specific pain. Comments = high intent.

---

### POST 2: Educational Value

> **Workers' Comp Audit Tip:** Your insurance carrier is asking for a valid COI for every sub who worked that year. Missing one isn't a minor issue — it gets flagged as uninsured payroll.
>
> At construction rates ($15-$20 per $100), that can cost you $6K-$8K per sub per year.
>
> If you're managing 5-10 subs, one lapsed policy is a five-figure surprise. How are you catching expirations before audit time?

**Why:** Educates, quantifies the pain, asks a question to spark discussion.

---

### POST 3: Process Question

> Question for the GCs in here: when you're prepping for your Workers' Comp audit, what's your biggest pain point?
>
> Is it:
> - Tracking down updated COIs from subs?
> - Organizing docs?
> - Keeping track of what's about to expire?
> - Something else?
>
> Genuinely curious how everyone handles this.

**Why:** Open-ended question gets comments. Comments = engagement = visibility for you.

---

### POST 4: Tactical Advice

> If you manage subs, here's something that saved me money:
>
> **Set calendar reminders 30 days before your audit** for three things:
> 1. Pull a list of every sub who worked that year
> 2. Check their COI expiry dates
> 3. Follow up on anyone within 30 days of expiring
>
> One audit, I missed this step. Got hit with a surprise bill.
>
> Most of you probably have a better system. What does your pre-audit checklist look like?

**Why:** Gives legitimate advice first. Admits failure. Asks question to build discussion.

---

### POST 5: Community Building

> Just connected with another contractor who was using a spreadsheet to track COIs for 15 subs. Took him 3 hours to audit-prep last month.
>
> It made me realize how many of us are doing this the hard way.
>
> What's the most time-consuming part of running your sub crew? For me it's compliance tracking. For others it seems to be invoicing or scheduling.

**Why:** Relatability. Opens door for people to vent. High comment rate.

## OUTREACH CADENCE & TRACKING

### LinkedIn Outreach
**Week 1:**
- Send 10-15 connection requests (use Variant 1 or 2)
- Personalize each based on profile

**Week 2-4:**
- Follow-ups to accepted connections
- Send DMs to 50% of acceptances (use Variant 1 for best conversion)
- Note: ~30-40% will respond

**Convert to demo if:**
- They admit pain (spreadsheets, agent-handled, nothing)
- They ask "how does it work"
- They mention upcoming audit

### Facebook Group Outreach
**Week 1-2:**
- Post 2-3 value posts (use POST 1, 3, 5)
- Engage authentically (comment on others' posts, answer questions)

**Week 2-4:**
- 1 value post every 3-4 days
- DM high-intent commenters (use Facebook DM template)
- Note: ~40-50% of DMs convert to signups

### Cold Email Outreach
**Week 1:**
- Build list of 50 target GCs (LinkedIn sales navigator or local search)
- Send 10 cold emails per day, Variants 1-3 rotated

**Week 2-4:**
- Send 5-10 emails per day
- Follow-up sequence: Day 5 ("checking in"), Day 10 ("one more thing")
- Expected response rate: 2-5%

### Insurance Agent Outreach
**Month 1:**
- Email 10-15 agents (use Agent Partnership Email)
- Follow up after 7 days if no response
- Expected conversion to partnership: 10-20%

## MESSAGING GUARDRAILS

**Always use this language:**
- "sub" (not "vendor")
- "COI" (not "certificate")
- "audit" (not "compliance review")
- "lapsed policy" (not "expired insurance")
- "audit bill" or "audit surprise" (not "liability")

**Never use:**
- "Leverage"
- "Utilize"
- "Vendor compliance"
- "Risk management workflow"
- "Innovative"

**Tone Checklist:**
- [ ] Sounds like a GC talking to a GC (not a salesman)
- [ ] Specific problem mentioned (not generic value prop)
- [ ] GC pain validated (before asking for anything)
- [ ] Under 100 words (mobile-friendly, busy people)
- [ ] One clear next step (link, question, or ask)
- [ ] Signed personally (Rishi, not "Team SubDocs")
