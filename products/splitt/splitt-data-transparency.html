Identity & Account Data

To create your account and comply with financial regulations, we need to know who you are. Payment products are required by law to verify user identities — this is called KYC (Know Your Customer) and it protects both you and the financial system from fraud.

Email address
e.g. you@gmail.com
Encrypted Never sold
Why we collect this
Your email is your account login and how we send you split receipts and security alerts. We do not use it for marketing unless you opt in. You can change or delete it at any time in Settings.
Full legal name
e.g. Jane Marie Smith
Encrypted Never sold
Why we collect this
Required by federal law (Bank Secrecy Act) to issue you a payment card. Your name appears on your Splitt. card and is verified against your government ID during onboarding. This is a legal requirement — we cannot issue cards without it.
Date of birth
e.g. January 15, 1990
Encrypted Never sold
Why we collect this
Required to verify you are 18 or older and to confirm your identity during KYC. We store only the date — not a copy of your ID document. This data is never used for anything beyond identity verification.
Mailing address
e.g. 123 Main St, Austin TX 78701
Encrypted Never sold
Why we collect this
Required for KYC compliance and to ship your physical Splitt. card. Also used for billing address verification when your card is used at merchants that check AVS (Address Verification System). We do not use your address for any marketing.
Last 4 digits of SSN
e.g. ••••••••• 4821
Encrypted Never sold
Why we collect this
Required by federal law for identity verification when issuing payment cards. We collect only the last 4 digits — never your full SSN. This is passed directly to our card issuing partner (Lithic) over an encrypted connection and is never stored in our own database.

Payment Information

The whole point of Splitt. is splitting purchases across your cards. Here's exactly what we see — and critically, what we don't.

Card brand, last 4 digits, expiry
e.g. Visa •••• 4242, expires 12/27
Encrypted Never sold
Why we collect this
We store only this summary so you can identify your cards in the app (e.g. "Visa ending in 4242"). Your full 16-digit card number is never stored by Splitt. It is tokenized by Stripe at the moment you enter it and we receive only a token reference.
Stripe payment method token
e.g. pm_1ABC23def456GHI
Encrypted Never sold
Why we collect this
This is a Stripe-generated reference ID for your card — not the card number itself. It's how we tell Stripe which card to charge for your split without ever handling your actual card details. This token is useless to anyone who doesn't also control your Stripe account.
Card nickname & split configuration
e.g. "Work Stipend — 40%, Chase Sapphire — 60%"
Never sold
Why we collect this
Your split rules are the core of the product — we store them so your preferences are applied automatically at every purchase. You can change, pause, or delete any split rule at any time.

Transaction Data

Every split generates a record so you can see exactly what happened, retry failed charges, and have evidence if there's ever a dispute with a merchant.

Merchant name & category
e.g. "Whole Foods Market — Grocery"
Never sold Deleted on request
Why we collect this
Shown in your transaction history so you know where each split occurred. Also used by the Rewards Optimizer to identify which of your cards earns the most for a given merchant category (e.g. 3x at grocery stores). We do not sell this to advertisers or data brokers.
Transaction amount & split breakdown
e.g. "$87.50 total — $25 from Stipend, $62.50 from Visa"
Encrypted Never sold
Why we collect this
The exact amounts charged to each card are logged per split leg. This is your record of what happened and our evidence if a secondary charge is disputed. If a charge fails, this record enables our automatic retry logic.
Transaction timestamp & status
e.g. "March 9, 2026 at 2:14 PM — Completed"
Never sold Deleted on request
Why we collect this
Timestamps create an audit trail. If you dispute a charge with your bank, the timestamp proves when the transaction occurred. Status tracking (completed, failed, partially failed) is how we know whether to notify you of a problem and trigger a retry.

Fraud & Security
Fraud Detection Data

This is the category that surprises most people — and the one worth explaining most carefully. Splitt. sits in a uniquely vulnerable position: we approve a transaction at the merchant before we've confirmed the secondary card will pay. Without fraud signals, bad actors could game this gap. Here's what we collect and exactly why.

Merchant location at time of purchase
e.g. "Austin, TX, United States"
Never sold Not your GPS
Why we collect this
This is the merchant's location, not yours. When a card authorization fires, the payment network tells us where the merchant terminal is located — this is standard data in every card transaction worldwide. We use it to detect impossible travel (e.g. a purchase in Austin and one in Tokyo 10 minutes apart), which is one of the clearest signals of card fraud. We do not use your phone's GPS. We do not track where you physically are.
Merchant Category Code (MCC)
e.g. MCC 5411 — Grocery Stores
Never sold
Why we collect this
Every merchant in the world has a 4-digit category code assigned by card networks. We use it to power your Rewards Optimizer (routing to the right card for maximum points) and to detect unusual patterns — for example, a sudden shift from normal grocery and dining spending to high-risk merchant categories like wire transfer services can indicate account takeover.
Transaction velocity & daily totals
e.g. "4 transactions today, $312.50 total"
Never sold Per-account only
Why we collect this
We track how many transactions you make per day and the total dollar amount. This isn't to spy on your spending — it's to protect you. If someone steals your Splitt. card and tries to run 30 transactions in a day, our velocity limits catch it and freeze further charges before the damage compounds. New accounts have tighter limits that relax as we build a history of your normal patterns.
Secondary card decline rate
e.g. "Card ending 9821: 0 failures in 23 charges"
Never sold
Why we collect this
We track how often your linked secondary cards fail when charged. Legitimate cards occasionally fail (expired, temporarily blocked) but a card that fails consistently is a signal worth examining. High failure rates on a secondary card may indicate a stolen card was added to your account — we flag this to protect you and other users on our platform.
Cardholder verification method
e.g. "Chip & PIN", "Contactless", "Online CNP"
Never sold
Why we collect this
Payment networks tell us how a transaction was verified — whether a chip was inserted, a PIN entered, or it was a tap. This is our primary dispute evidence. If you're ever wrongly charged for a transaction you didn't make, the verification method proves physical card possession at the time of purchase.

Functional
Device & Technical Data

Standard data collected by the app to make it work reliably and to diagnose problems when something breaks.

Device type & operating system
e.g. iPhone 15 Pro, iOS 18.2
Anonymized Never sold
Why we collect this
Used to diagnose app crashes and compatibility issues. If a bug only affects iPhone users on a specific iOS version, we need to know that to fix it. This is collected at the model level — we do not collect your device's unique serial number or IMEI.
Push notification token
e.g. ExponentPushToken[xxxxxxxxxxxx]
Never sold Deleted on logout
Why we collect this
The only way to send you an instant split confirmation or fraud alert is via push notification. This token is a device identifier used exclusively to route notifications to your phone. It is deleted when you log out and cannot be used to identify you across apps.
App crash logs & error reports
e.g. "NullPointerException in chargeService.js line 84"
Anonymized Never sold
Why we collect this
When something goes wrong — a charge fails unexpectedly, the app crashes, a webhook misfires — we need to know about it immediately to fix it. Error logs are sent to Sentry (our monitoring tool) and contain the technical error, not your personal data. We strip any personal identifiers before errors are logged.

Hard limits
What We Never Do

These are not aspirations. They are hard technical and contractual constraints built into how Splitt. operates.

Sell your data
Your personal information and spending data are never sold to data brokers, advertisers, or any third party for any reason.
Store full card numbers
We never see or store your 16-digit card number, CVV, or PIN. Card data goes directly to Stripe's PCI-compliant vault — not our servers.
Track your GPS location
We do not request, access, or store your phone's real-time GPS coordinates. Location signals in fraud detection come from merchant terminal data, not your device.
Use data for advertising
We run no ad network integrations. Your spending patterns are never used to build advertising profiles or target you with third-party ads.
Share data without consent
Data is shared only with the infrastructure partners listed below that are required to operate the product. No exceptions without your explicit consent.
Store full SSN
We collect only the last 4 digits of your SSN for identity verification, passed directly to Lithic over an encrypted connection. Your full SSN is never stored anywhere by Splitt.

Infrastructure Partners
Who Else Sees Your Data

We use a small set of carefully chosen infrastructure partners. Each one sees only the data they need to do their job — nothing more.

Lithic
Card issuing
Issues your physical Splitt. card. Sees your name, address, date of birth, and last 4 of SSN for KYC compliance. Processes authorization webhooks. Governed by their own privacy policy and banking regulations.
Stripe
Payment processing
Tokenizes and charges your linked secondary cards. Stores your full card details in their PCI-compliant vault — Splitt. never accesses these. Also handles subscription billing.
Supabase
Database
Hosts your account data, split rules, and transaction history on encrypted servers. Based on PostgreSQL. Data is stored in the United States.
Sentry
Error monitoring
Receives anonymized error reports when the app or backend experiences a problem. Personal identifiers are stripped before errors are sent. Used only to diagnose and fix bugs.
Resend
Email delivery
Sends transactional emails: split receipts, security alerts, and account notifications. Sees your email address only. Does not have access to your financial data.
Intercom
Customer support
Powers the in-app help chat. When you contact support, we share your name, email, and account ID so agents can help you. Chat transcripts are retained for support quality purposes.
