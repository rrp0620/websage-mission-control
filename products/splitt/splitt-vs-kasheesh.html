Core Mechanic
Advantage

User must open app before each purchase, set the split, generate a virtual card number, then go use that number at checkout. The multi-use card is "set it and forget it" but percentage-only — no smart routing per purchase.

Advantage

Pricing Model
Advantage

Monthly subscription (Free / Plus $4.99 / Pro $9.99). No per-transaction fee. Zero purchase-moment friction.
Per-Transaction

2% fee on every transaction processed. 1–1.5% rewards back, so net cost ~0.5–1%. Users feel the fee every single time they use it — creates hesitation on smaller purchases.
Split Logic
Fixed amount, percentage, remainder, and rules-engine routing by merchant category, time of day, or amount. Up to 5 cards per split.
Percentage-only on the multi-use card. Fixed amounts available on single-use cards only. No rules engine or intelligent routing. One user noted "unless you have a degree in math, this app isn't for you" — percentage UX is confusing.
Card Types
Credit, debit, FSA/HSA, employer stipends, prepaid — anything Stripe can tokenize.
Credit and debit via Plaid. Prepaid gift cards — advertised but often doesn't work. Multiple users complained gift cards cannot be verified. Several had accounts revoked for using gift cards the platform claims to support.
Onboarding
Guided 4-step flow. Add card via Stripe Elements (no Plaid dependency). Simpler verification path.
Major Pain Point

ID scanning frequently fails. Plaid verification blocks many users. Multiple users could not get past sign-up. One reported a 20-minute crash loop just to onboard.
In-Store Usage
Physical Stripe-issued debit card. Tap anywhere. Split happens invisibly in the background.
Supports tap-to-pay via Apple/Google Pay but card is pinless — must select "Credit" not "Debit" or it fails. Users report embarrassing declines at registers. Support is email-only, useless in an in-store emergency.
Account Control
Standard account management. Delete cards, update rules, cancel subscription.
Serious Issue

Multiple users report being unable to remove linked cards. No account deletion path visible in app. One user said searching "close account" returns an error. This creates real security concerns.
Transaction Visibility
Full transaction history with expandable split legs per charge. Per-card status (completed, failed, partially failed). Retry on failure.
Basic transaction history. Users reported not being notified when transactions failed, discovering problems only weeks later.

Email-only. Average response time claimed 90 min, but users report waiting a week or more with no response. No phone, no chat, no real-time help.
Brand Trust

Trustpilot 2.9/5. Multiple "does not do what is advertised" complaints. Users suspect fake positive reviews. Some Glassdoor employees describe leadership as unstable and a Series A that never closed after 4 years of promises.

What Kasheesh Gets Right
Proven Market Demand
Tens of thousands of users and millions in transactions processed in 2024 — despite a broken product. This proves the demand is real and people will push through friction to get this capability.
Browser Extension Distribution
The Chrome extension means they show up at the point of intent — on the merchant's page. Users don't have to switch apps. Positive browser reviews are their best feedback, suggesting this is the right channel to reach power users.
Apple Pay / Google Pay Integration
The multi-use card can be added to digital wallets. For online-only users this is genuinely convenient and reduces the friction of entering card numbers. Splitt. should target this too.
Use Case Discovery
Their content marketing (100+ SEO articles on specific use cases) has surfaced the exact scenarios customers care about: tax payments, rent, Costco, Amazon, flights. This is a roadmap of what Splitt. should message against.
Where Splitt. Is Currently Lacking — Honest Assessment
Gap Why It Matters What to Do About It Priority
Zero market proof Kasheesh has tens of thousands of users and real transaction volume. Splitt. has a waitlist. Every investor, partner, and early adopter will ask "who's using it?" First 50 real users transacting is the only fix. Speed to beta matters more than polish right now. Critical
No live customer support plan Kasheesh's #1 failure is email-only support that goes dark. Payments are high-stakes — a declined card in-store is a crisis. Users need a fast path to resolution. Even a well-staffed Intercom chat or a clear real-time status page would differentiate. Plan this before launch, not after. Critical
No browser presence The browser extension is Kasheesh's best-rated distribution channel. Splitt. has no equivalent online presence beyond the card. Phase 3 checkout button solves this — but consider a lightweight browser extension even in Phase 2 to reach online-only users. Medium
Stripe Issuing geographic limits Stripe Issuing is US-only. Kasheesh is also US-only currently, so this is parity — but it caps the TAM and must be acknowledged. Not a launch blocker, but be transparent about it. Monitor Stripe's international Issuing expansion. Low
No rewards layer at launch Kasheesh has a 1–1.5% cashback rewards program. It's a retention tool that makes the 2% fee feel more palatable. Splitt. has no loyalty hook yet. Subscription tiers partially address this (value is in features, not cashback). But a rewards optimizer page (already in the product spec) should ship early, not late. Medium
Stripe Issuing approval dependency The entire product is blocked on Stripe Issuing approval. If that drags, nothing ships. Kasheesh had the same dependency and got through it — but it's a real risk. Document the use case clearly for Stripe reviewers. Emphasize consumer-facing, low-risk spending. Have backup communication ready. Critical
No social proof or press yet Kasheesh has VC backing (Tribe Capital), celebrity investors, and Business Wire coverage. Splitt. is invisible to media and investors right now. One good Product Hunt launch, one fintech newsletter feature, or one angel check changes this. Start the outreach now, not after launch. Medium
The Bottom Line

Kasheesh proved the market. Splitt. can own it.

Kasheesh's existence is the best thing that could have happened to Splitt. They've done three years of consumer education, built tens of thousands of users who now know what multi-card splitting is, and collected a mountain of negative reviews that serve as a precise product spec for what not to do.

The core failure of Kasheesh is architectural: they built a manual product for an automatic problem. Every interaction requires the user to think, configure, and act before a purchase. Splitt.'s webhook-intercept model means the user never has to think at all — which is how every great payments product ultimately wins.

The honest gaps for Splitt. right now are not product gaps — they're market gaps. No users, no press, no social proof, and a launch blocked on approvals. Kasheesh got to market first and it still hasn't figured out basic reliability. Splitt.'s opportunity is to be the version of this product that actually works.
