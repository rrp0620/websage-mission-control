Brand Foundation
Why Splitt.
is built this way
Every design decision has a deliberate reason. Here's the thinking behind the brand.
.
The Dot Is the Brand
The period at the end of Splitt. is intentional punctuation. It signals finality — the split is done, the purchase is handled. It's a confident full stop in a category full of vague, open-ended names. "Splitt." reads like a complete thought. That's the product promise: you tap, it's done, period.
■
Black as the Foundation
Deep black (#0a0a0a) communicates trust, weight, and control — the language of premium cards, trading terminals, and Bloomberg. We're a product that touches your money. Black signals we take that seriously. Not pure #000 — slightly warm, slightly more refined.
★
Electric Lime as the Action Color
#c8ff57 is not a typical fintech color — that's exactly why we chose it. Every competitor uses blue. Lime signals energy, growth, and your money working harder. It's the color of "go." Every CTA, confirmation, and success state uses lime. Viscerally associated with action.
◇
Cyan as the Second Voice
#57c8ff plays the supporting role — used for secondary card highlights and informational states. It's the visual "second card" to lime's "first card." Together they create a palette that's unmistakably Splitt. and impossible to confuse with any existing fintech brand.
A
Syne for Display
Syne is a geometric display typeface with unusual letter proportions that feel distinctly modern without being trendy. Its heavy weight at large sizes has real personality — designed for posters and headlines, which is exactly how we use it. Every big number, every brand moment, every heading.
a
DM Sans for Readability
DM Sans was designed for small-size usage on screen — optimized for legibility in interfaces. Where Syne grabs attention, DM Sans delivers information cleanly. Its light weight at body sizes creates elegant contrast with Syne's heavy display presence.
Logo
The Wordmark
Always set in Syne ExtraBold (800 weight). The dot is always present — it's not decorative punctuation, it's core to the brand identity. Never remove it.
